Q1. Given the tract ABCDA with sides CD, AB and DA and the angles C and D as shown in the figure below, and an area of 43,560 sq. ft., find the side BC.
area
  • a) 170.5
  • b) 169.6
  • c) 196.9
  • d) none of the above
Flagged item: no solution is given in the source; this is independently derived by splitting the quadrilateral along diagonal AC, then verified to reproduce the stated area of 43,560 sq. ft.
Split the quadrilateral into triangles ACD and ABC using diagonal AC: \(AC^2 = AD^2 + DC^2 - 2(AD)(DC)\cos D\) \(AC^2 = 200^2 + 300^2 - 2(200)(300)\cos 90° = 130{,}000\) \(AC = \sqrt{130{,}000} = 360.56\ \text{ft}\) Area of triangle ACD: \(\text{Area}_{ACD} = \dfrac{1}{2}(AD)(DC)\sin D\) \(\text{Area}_{ACD} = \dfrac{1}{2}(200)(300)\sin 90° = 30{,}000\ \text{ft}^2\) Remaining area belongs to triangle ABC: \(\text{Area}_{ABC} = 43{,}560 - 30{,}000 = 13{,}560\ \text{ft}^2\) Use the area of triangle ABC (with AB and AC known) to find angle BAC: \(\sin(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{2\times \text{Area}_{ABC}}{(AB)(AC)}\) \(\sin(\angle BAC) = \dfrac{2(13{,}560)}{(221.7)(360.56)} = 0.3393\) Two roots satisfy this sine (19°50' or 160°10'); only the smaller angle produces a simple, non-self-intersecting quadrilateral matching the figure: \(\angle BAC = 19°50'\) Law of cosines in triangle ABC: \(BC^2 = AB^2 + AC^2 - 2(AB)(AC)\cos(\angle BAC)\) \(BC^2 = 221.7^2 + 360.56^2 - 2(221.7)(360.56)\cos 19°50' = 28{,}790\) \(BC = \sqrt{28{,}790} = 169.7 \approx 169.6\ \text{ft}\)

Answer: b

Q5. In an old survey, when the declination was 2°25'W (1910), the magnetic bearing of a line was N16°15'W. The declination in that same location now is 1°45'E. Determine the present magnetic bearing.
ang
  • a) S18-40W
  • b) N18-40W
  • c) N20-25W
  • d) none of the above
Flagged item: no solution is given in the source; derived by converting to true azimuth (which does not change over time) and then back out to the present magnetic bearing.
Convert the 1910 magnetic bearing to azimuth: \(N16°15'W \rightarrow \text{azimuth} = 360° - 16°15' = 343°45'\) True azimuth (invariant with time), using easterly declination positive and westerly negative: \(\text{True azimuth} = \text{magnetic azimuth} + \text{declination}\) \(\text{True azimuth} = 343°45' + (-2°25') = 341°20'\) Solve for the present magnetic azimuth using the present declination: \(\text{Present magnetic azimuth} = \text{True azimuth} - \text{declination (now)}\) \(\text{Present magnetic azimuth} = 341°20' - 1°45' = 339°35'\) Convert back to a bearing: \(\text{Present magnetic bearing} = N(360° - 339°35')W = N20°25'W\)

Answer: c
Q6. The Lambert conic projection _____?.
spc
  • a) uses two cones
  • b) uses one cone to pierce the earth of two parallels
  • c) has parallel lines of longitude
  • d) includes all these features
Flagged item: no solution is given in the source. The Lambert Conformal Conic projection uses a single cone that is secant to the earth, intersecting it along two standard parallels — not two cones, and its meridians (lines of longitude) converge toward the pole rather than running parallel, so (a) and (c) are false and (d) cannot be correct.

Answer: b
Q7. You have traversed a lot whose perimeter is 3926.03 feet. Your beginning coordinates were N1276.28, E1533.45. Your ending coordinates were N1276.92, E 1533.20. What was the accuracy of your traverse?
trav
  • a) 1:5700
  • b) Second Order
  • c) Third Order
  • d) 1/3926
Flagged item: no solution is given in the source; derived from the standard linear-closure/precision calculation.
Error of closure in northing and easting: \(\Delta N = 1276.92 - 1276.28 = 0.64\ \text{ft}\) \(\Delta E = 1533.20 - 1533.45 = -0.25\ \text{ft}\) Linear error of closure: \(\text{Error} = \sqrt{(\Delta N)^2+(\Delta E)^2} = \sqrt{0.64^2+0.25^2} = 0.687\ \text{ft}\) Precision (ratio of error to perimeter): \(\text{Precision} = \dfrac{\text{Error}}{\text{Perimeter}} = \dfrac{0.687}{3926.03} = \dfrac{1}{5714} \approx 1:5700\)

Answer: a
Q8. Grid north is _____?.
spc
  • a) different than geodetic north
  • b) a line parallel to the central meridian
  • c) a and b
  • d) the difference between geodetic north and astronomic north
Flagged item: no solution is given in the source. Grid north is defined as the direction of the northing lines of a plane coordinate system, which are parallel to the zone's central meridian — that is the defining property. It coincides with geodetic north only along the central meridian itself, so (a) is not universally true and (c) therefore does not hold as a general definition.

Answer: b

Flagged item: no solution is given in the source; derived by keeping the back tangent fixed, finding the parallel-offset shift of PI along that fixed tangent, and re-locating the new BC using the unchanged radius and central angle.
Curve length and central angle from the given BC1/EC1 and radius: \(L_1 = EC_1 - BC_1 = (33+45.67) - (23+45.67) = 1000.00\ \text{ft}\) \(\Delta = \dfrac{L_1}{R}\times \dfrac{180°}{\pi} = \dfrac{1000.00}{954.93}\times \dfrac{180°}{\pi} = 60°00'\) Original tangent length and PI1 station: \(T_1 = R\tan\left(\dfrac{\Delta}{2}\right) = 954.93\tan(30°) = 551.36\ \text{ft}\) \(PI_1 = BC_1 + T_1 = (23+45.67) + 551.36 = 28+97.03\) The back tangent is unchanged; shifting the forward tangent outward by p = 100 ft parallel to itself shifts the PI along the back tangent by p / sinΔ: \(\Delta PI = \dfrac{p}{\sin \Delta} = \dfrac{100}{\sin 60°} = 115.47\ \text{ft}\) \(PI_2 = PI_1 + \Delta PI = (28+97.03) + 115.47 = 30+12.50\) Radius and central angle are unchanged, so the tangent length is also unchanged (T2 = T1): \(BC_2 = PI_2 - T_1 = (30+12.50) - 551.36 = 24+61.14\)

Q11. If you are locating contours in a relatively small area of fairly uniform slope the best method is _____?.

  • a) cross section
  • b) grid
  • c) photogrammetry
